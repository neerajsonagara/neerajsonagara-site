EPC to operations handover: why engineering information goes wrong and how to fix it

By Neeraj Sonagara·Digital Twin & Digital Transformation Consultant·5 min read

A new facility can be mechanically complete and still start up blind, because the engineering information that operations needs is missing, inconsistent or unusable.

Handover is where a Digital Twin is either given a clean start or inherits years of problems. I have validated EPC deliverables ahead of operational handover and supported commissioning readiness, and the same issues repeat.

Why handover goes wrong

  • Data requirements arrive too late. Contractors build to drawings, not to the data operations will need.
  • Tag and equipment naming differs between contractors and between projects.
  • Documents are not linked to tags, so they cannot be found from the asset.
  • As-built and records disagree because late changes were never reflected.
  • Formats do not fit the target system, so data has to be rebuilt by hand.
  • No one validates before acceptance, so errors are found after start-up.

What works

  1. Define the data requirements in the contract. State what data, in what structure, to which standard. CFIHOS is a good starting point.
  2. Agree one tag and equipment register early and keep it controlled.
  3. Validate continuously, not once at the end. Automate checks so quality holds at volume.
  4. Set quality gates before acceptance. Data that fails does not pass.
  5. Have the target system ready to receive structured data, and link documents to tags as they load.
  6. Give operations ownership of the information from handover day.

Treat handover data as a deliverable with acceptance criteria, exactly like the plant itself.

Why this matters for a Digital Twin

A clean handover means the Digital Twin starts accurate instead of being cleaned up for years. See the 10 common Digital Twin challenges and what a Digital Twin is in oil & gas.
