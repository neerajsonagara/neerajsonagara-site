IT/OT convergence in oil & gas: why it is hard and how to start

By Neeraj Sonagara·Digital Twin & Digital Transformation Consultant·5 min read

IT and OT run the same company with different priorities. Joining them is less a technology problem than an agreement problem about data, ownership and risk.

Two worldsDifferent goals, tools and risk
Start smallOne use case, read-only data first
Shared modelOne asset hierarchy for both sides

Why it is hard

  • Different priorities. OT protects safety and availability. IT protects data and cost.
  • Different tag structures. A control-system tag and a maintenance equipment ID rarely match.
  • Silos. Historians, SCADA and DCS sit apart from CMMS, ERP and engineering systems.
  • Security concerns. Connecting control networks to enterprise networks needs care.
  • No joint ownership. Nobody is responsible for the data between the two.

How to start

  1. Pick one use case with clear value, for example condition data for maintenance decisions.
  2. Agree a shared asset hierarchy and tag model that maps control tags to equipment and documents.
  3. Move data one way first. Read from OT into an integration layer before considering anything that writes back.
  4. Use open interfaces such as OPC UA, MQTT and REST APIs through a governed layer.
  5. Protect the boundary. Segment networks and apply recognised OT security practice such as IEC 62443.
  6. Set shared governance so IT and OT agree who owns quality, access and change.

Real-time data without asset context is just numbers. The value comes from tying it to the equipment, documents and maintenance history around it.

How it connects to a Digital Twin

A Digital Twin is where the two sides meet: live operational data placed in the context of engineering and business information. See Digital Twin vs digital model and the 10 common challenges.
