Why Digital Twin pilots never scale (and how to avoid pilot purgatory)

By Neeraj Sonagara·Digital Twin & Digital Transformation Consultant·5 min read

Many Digital Twin pilots impress in a demo and then stop. The pilot proved the technology could work. It never proved the organisation could run it.

Demo trapPilots run on hand-cleaned data
RepeatableDesign the pilot to be copied
Operating modelOwnership and support decide scale

Why pilots stall

  • The data was cleaned by hand for the pilot, and no one can repeat that at scale.
  • The platform was chosen for the demo, not for the enterprise.
  • There is no operating model: no owner, no support, no budget after the pilot.
  • Integration is one-off, not a pattern other assets can reuse.
  • Value was never measured, so there is nothing to justify the next step.

How to design a pilot that scales

  1. Choose a representative asset, not the easiest one.
  2. Use real data and real integrations, built as a repeatable pattern.
  3. Build the information model once, so it can be reused across assets.
  4. Define success in numbers before you start: time saved, errors avoided, decisions improved.
  5. Plan the roll-out from day one, including ownership, governance and support.
  6. Train users and embed the tool in a daily workflow.

A pilot is not a smaller version of the project. It is the first copy of something you intend to repeat.

What scale looks like

Scaling a Digital Twin across many business units, systems and thousands of users depends far more on information discipline and governance than on the platform. See the whitepaper and why programs fail without an information foundation.
