Why Digital Twin programs fail without an information foundation

By Neeraj Sonagara·Digital Twin & Digital Transformation Consultant·5 min read

When a Digital Twin program disappoints, the technology is rarely the cause. The platform works. The problem is that it was switched on top of information nobody trusted.

Not softwareFailures come from the information beneath
5 causesNo truth source, no owner, IT/OT gap and more
Fix firstFoundation before the viewer

Across enterprise programs involving many business units, dozens of systems and thousands of users, the pattern is the same. The visible part, the viewer or the dashboard, gets the attention. The invisible part, the quality and ownership of the data underneath, decides whether the program succeeds.

1. Starting with software instead of information

Choosing a platform first feels like progress. But a platform only displays what it is given. If the equipment lists, tags and documents are inconsistent, the Digital Twin will show that inconsistency, just more beautifully.

2. No single source of truth

The same pump appears under three tag names in three systems, and two drawings disagree about its connections. Until one governed record exists for each asset, every answer has to be double-checked, and people go back to their old spreadsheets.

3. Nobody owns the data

Information decays when it has no owner. Without named data owners, quality rules and a process for change, even a clean migration slowly drifts back into disorder.

4. IT and OT never really meet

Engineering and enterprise systems sit on one side, control and operational systems on the other. A Digital Twin that only shows static engineering data, or only live sensor data, answers half the question. The value is in joining them through a governed integration layer.

5. Chasing demos instead of use cases

An impressive 3D demo wins a budget but not a business case. Programs that last start from a use case with a measurable outcome, for example faster information retrieval, better turnaround planning or cleaner project handover, and work backwards to the data needed.

A useful test: if you removed the visual layer tomorrow, would the underlying information still be trusted, connected and owned? If not, the foundation is the project.

What to do instead

  1. Assess readiness first. Measure the quality and structure of the information before choosing a solution.
  2. Define the information model and standards. Standards first, software second.
  3. Clean and migrate with quality gates. Automate validation so quality holds at volume.
  4. Assign ownership and governance before go-live, not after.
  5. Connect IT and OT through one governed layer, then prioritise use cases by value.
  6. Train and support users. A Digital Twin nobody uses delivers nothing.
