Digital Twin vs digital model: what actually creates business value?

By Neeraj Sonagara·Digital Twin & Digital Transformation Consultant·4 min read

Many projects are called Digital Twins when they are really digital models. The difference is not just vocabulary. It decides whether you get a nice picture or a tool people make decisions with.

ModelStatic picture, updated by hand
ShadowLive data flowing one way
TwinConnected, governed, decision-ready

Definitions vary by vendor, but in common industry usage the three terms separate along one question: how does information move between the physical asset and its digital version?

Digital modelDigital shadowDigital Twin
Data flowNone. Built once, updated by hand.One way: the physical asset feeds the digital copy.Continuous and connected, with context and relationships.
Stays current?Only if someone updates it.Yes, for the data it receives.Yes, and the information behind it is governed.
AnswersWhat does it look like?What is happening now?What is happening, why, and what should we do?
Typical example3D model, drawing setLive dashboard of sensor dataConnected engineering, operations and maintenance information

Why the distinction matters for value

A digital model is valuable for design and visualisation, but it ages the day it is finished. A digital shadow is valuable for monitoring, but it only shows readings, not meaning. A Digital Twin earns its name when engineering information, live operational data and business context are tied together, so people can act on what they see.

Three questions to test what you have

  1. Does it update itself? If it relies on manual refreshes, it is a model.
  2. Is it connected to operational data? If not, it cannot tell you what is happening now.
  3. Is anyone making decisions from it? If nobody does, it has no business value yet, whatever it is called.

These are stages on a path, not rivals. A good model is the starting point, live data turns it into a shadow, and governed, connected information turns it into a Digital Twin.

The foundation is the same in all three

Whichever stage you are at, the same thing limits you: the quality, structure and ownership of the information underneath. That is why I start every engagement with the information foundation. See why Digital Twin programs fail without it, or read the whitepaper.
